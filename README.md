# Key Metrics for Assessing the Health of a Mobile Free-to-Play Game

No single metric captures the overall health of a free-to-play game. I'd assess five key areas to understand the game's performance:

- Do players stay?
- Do they engage?
- Does the game generate revenue?
- Is the virtual economy balanced?
- Where is the game breaking down?

### 1. Retention and XURR's: Is the game worth coming back to?

Key Metrics

- Day 1, Day 7, and Day 30 Retention .
- Typical benchmarks:
  - D1: 35–40%
  - D7: 15–20%
  - D30: 5–8%

 
Why It Matters

- D1 reflects the quality of the first-time user experience.
- D7 reflects the depth of the early game.
- D30 reflects long-term content and meta-progression.
- NURR/CURR/RURR helps distinguish whether the game is retaining new users, existing users, or reactivating inactive users.
  
### 2. Engagement and Progression: How deeply are players engaging with the game?

Key Metrics

- DAU (Daily Active Users): Number of unique players who play the game daily.
- MAU (Monthly Active Users): Number of unique players who play the game monthly.
- FTUE Completion Rate: Percentage of new players who complete the First-Time User Experience.
- Stickiness (DAU ÷ MAU): Above roughly 20% is generally healthy, and often higher for social games.
- Session Length: Average time players spend in a single gaming session.
- Sessions per Day: Average number of sessions played by each player daily.

Why It Matters

- Shows how strongly the game is becoming part of a player's daily habit.
- Short, frequent sessions usually indicate a strong habit loop.
- Long but infrequent sessions can signal content exhaustion or a weak reason to return.

### 3. Monetization: Where does the money come from, and can we count on it long-term?

Key Metrics

- ARPDAU (Average Revenue Per Daily Active User).
- Conversion Rate: Percentage of players who make at least one purchase.
- ARPPU (Average Revenue Per Paying User): Average revenue per paying user.
- LTV (Lifetime Value): Total  revenue generated by a player throughout their lifetime.

Why It Matters

- Free-to-play revenue is heavily skewed, so how the game earns matters as much as how much it earns.
- Low conversion with very high ARPPU means dependence on a few high-spending players (whales), making revenue volatile.

### 4. In-Game Economy: How is the net economy flow being maintained?

Key Metrics

- Currency Inflow and Outflow: Tracks how much virtual currency enters and leaves the game through rewards, purchases, upgrades, and other activities.
- Currency Balance (By Percentile): Measures the currency held by players at the percentile to identify excessive accumulation.

Why It Matters

- Maintain Economic Balance: Ensures currency entering the game is balanced against currency leaving the game.
- Prevent Inflation: Identifies situations where players accumulate excessive currency, reducing the value of rewards and purchases.

### 5. Device Performance and Load Time: How well does the game perform across different devices?

Key Metrics

- Average Game Launch Time: How long the game takes to open and become ready to play.
- Initial Loading Time : How long players wait when loading the game for the first time or launching it from a closed state.
- Level Loading Time: How long it takes to load a new level before players can start playing.

Why It Matters

- Helps identify performance issues across different devices.
- Longer loading times can frustrate players and increase drop-off rates(Friction point).
- Consistent performance provides a smoother gaming experience and improves player satisfaction.
